# Educational difficulty in a linear-equation progression

Context: [Research educational difficulty in linear-equation progression](https://github.com/quarion/poke-math/issues/12), part of [Shape a capability-based equation generation progression](https://github.com/quarion/poke-math/issues/4).

## Resolution

The capability model should use a learner-action progression as its spine and record representation, number domain, equation structure, and solution-path demands as separate dimensions. A single score derived from operator count, operand magnitude, or the printed length of an equation will not be educationally credible: official standards advance through different actions, while empirical studies show independent effects from unknown position, symbolic versus verbal presentation, number type, variable terms, negative terms, and interactions among equation elements. [Common Core State Standards, pp. 43, 48, 51–55](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf); [Koedinger and Nathan, 2004, pp. 142–144](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Koedinger%2CNathan-04.pdf); [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 2–7](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf); [Ngu, Chung, and Yeung, 2015](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1056287).

Standards should be treated as evidence for a prerequisite order, not as grade labels for this product. England's statutory curriculum explicitly allows content to be introduced earlier or later within a key stage, and the two standards examined group some capabilities differently while preserving the same broad movement from arithmetic fluency, through expressions and equivalence, to one-variable equations and then pairs of linear equations. [Department for Education, mathematics programmes of study, paragraphs 104–108](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study); [Common Core State Standards, pp. 27–28, 43, 48, 51–55](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf).

## Learner actions identified by standards

The standards expose a progression of distinct learner actions rather than a progression of larger numbers alone:

1. Compute and interpret operations: evaluate one- and multi-operation numerical expressions, distinguish additive from multiplicative relationships, respect order of operations, and assess whether an answer is reasonable. Common Core introduces multi-step four-operation problems with letters standing for unknown quantities before formal equation solving; England similarly moves from whole-number fluency to mixed operations and order of operations. [Common Core State Standards, pp. 27–28](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf); [Department for Education, key-stage 1 focus and years 5–6 number content](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study).
2. Read, construct, and evaluate expressions: treat a letter as a number, identify nested subexpressions, substitute values, and use properties such as distribution to construct equivalent expressions. These are explicit grade-6 Common Core actions and precede its formal multi-step linear-equation work. [Common Core State Standards, p. 43](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf).
3. Reason relationally about equality and unknowns: determine which values make an equation true, understand a variable as either a particular unknown or a member of a set, and verify by substitution. Common Core makes these explicit before one-step forms such as `x + p = q` and `px = q`. [Common Core State Standards, p. 43](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf).
4. Preserve equivalence while isolating an unknown: use inverse operations, apply the same valid transformation to an equation, and explain why each step preserves the solution. Common Core requires solving and reasoning about increasingly complex equations; England requires inverse-operation knowledge, equivalence-preserving manipulation, and rearrangement. [Common Core State Standards, pp. 48, 51, 53–54, 64](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf); [Department for Education, key-stage 3 number and algebra](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study).
5. Expose and use structure: collect like terms, distribute over brackets, simplify either side, and choose an efficient sequence of transformations, including equations with variable terms on both sides. Common Core explicitly includes expansion and collection of like terms; England lists collection, expansion, factoring, and all one-variable forms requiring rearrangement. [Common Core State Standards, pp. 53–54](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf); [Department for Education, key-stage 3 algebra](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study).
6. Coordinate multiple constraints and representations: interpret two equations as simultaneous conditions, solve a pair algebraically, and connect the solution to the intersection of two graphs. Common Core focuses its first systems expectation on two equations in two variables and asks for both algebraic solution and graphical estimation; England also connects linear equations, graphs, and approximate simultaneous solutions. [Common Core State Standards, pp. 51 and 54](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf); [Department for Education, key-stage 3 algebra](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study).

## Representations are capabilities, not decoration

Relevant representations include numerical expressions, missing-value equations, symbolic expressions and equations, verbal or contextual relationships, tables, and graphs. Standards ask learners to translate or relate several of these forms, including equations with verbal comparisons, variables representing quantities, and equations, tables, and graphs describing the same relationship. [Common Core State Standards, pp. 28, 43, 51 and 54](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf); [Department for Education, key-stage 3 algebra](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study).

Representation must not be assigned a universal difficulty rank. In two difficulty-factor assessments with beginning algebra students, Koedinger and Nathan found significant, separable effects for representation, unknown position, and number type; students were less successful on matched symbolic equations than on verbal forms, start-unknown items were harder than result-unknown items, and decimals were harder than whole numbers. The result means that the generator should label representation and unknown position explicitly and calibrate their effects empirically instead of assuming that a symbolic equation is the easiest form. [Koedinger and Nathan, 2004, pp. 142–144](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Koedinger%2CNathan-04.pdf).

Relational understanding of `=` is a prerequisite capability rather than a formatting detail. In a study of 177 middle-school students, equal-sign understanding was associated with successful equation solving, and the association remained in a smaller subsample after controlling for standardized mathematics achievement and other measured abilities. [Knuth, Stephens, McNeil, and Alibali, 2006, pp. 303–312](https://psych.wisc.edu/alibali/home/Publications_files/Knuth_Stephens_McNeil_Alibali_JRME_2006.pdf).

## Sources of cognitive complexity

The model should record at least the following independent sources of difficulty:

- Required learner action: compute, substitute, recognize equivalence, invert an operation, collect like terms, distribute, move a constant term, move a variable term, or coordinate equations. Common Core and England enumerate these as distinct expectations, and Long and colleagues' cognitive task analysis likewise separates equation solving into transformations and simplifications rather than treating it as one undifferentiated skill. [Common Core State Standards, pp. 43, 48 and 53–54](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf); [Department for Education, key-stage 3 algebra](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study); [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 2–3](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf).
- Unknown placement and occurrence: a result-unknown computation, a start-unknown equation, one occurrence on one side, repeated occurrences, and occurrences on both sides do not make equivalent demands. Koedinger and Nathan measured a reliable start-unknown penalty, while Long and colleagues observed a pronounced error increase when learners moved from `ax + b = c` to the superficially similar `ax + b = cx`. [Koedinger and Nathan, 2004, pp. 142–144](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Koedinger%2CNathan-04.pdf); [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 6–7](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf).
- Number domain and sign: whole numbers, integers, rational values, and negative constants or coefficients must be tagged independently of algebraic form. Koedinger and Nathan found number type affected performance; Long and colleagues found operations on negative terms tended to be harder than on positive terms, with negative variable terms harder than negative constants in their tutor data. [Koedinger and Nathan, 2004, pp. 142–144](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Koedinger%2CNathan-04.pdf); [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 5–7](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf).
- Structural and interactive load: term count, unlike terms, nesting, variables on both sides, and the number of simultaneously relevant relationships affect the choices and information a learner must coordinate. Long and colleagues found that a model specialized by equation complexity predicted tutor performance better than a fully abstract skill model; Ngu and colleagues' randomized experiment found method-dependent performance and cognitive-load differences on equations with high element interactivity. [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 3 and 6–7](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf); [Ngu, Chung, and Yeung, 2015](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1056287).
- Representation and translation: symbolic, verbal, contextual, tabular, and graphical forms may cue different comprehension and solution processes, even when their mathematical relationship is equivalent. Both standards require connections among representations, and Koedinger and Nathan directly measured representation effects. [Common Core State Standards, pp. 43, 51 and 54](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf); [Koedinger and Nathan, 2004, pp. 129–164](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Koedinger%2CNathan-04.pdf).
- Solution-path demand: the number and type of necessary transformations, the amount of state that must be maintained, and the availability of a much shorter shortcut matter more than printed size by itself. This is an inference from standards that require successive equivalence-preserving transformations and research separating the difficulty of particular transformations under different equation structures. [Common Core State Standards, pp. 51, 53–54 and 64](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf); [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 2–7](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf); [Ngu, Chung, and Yeung, 2015](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1056287).

## Constraints for the capability model

1. Define a capability level by its target learner action and prerequisite capabilities, never directly by grade, maximum value, operator allow-list, or equation length. Grade-level standards are evidence for ordering, not product labels. [Department for Education, paragraphs 104–108](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study); [Common Core State Standards, pp. 43, 48 and 51–55](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf).
2. Keep the action spine separate from at least four orthogonal profiles: number domain and sign, algebraic structure, representation, and solution-path demand. Empirical effects for those dimensions are not interchangeable. [Koedinger and Nathan, 2004, pp. 142–144](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Koedinger%2CNathan-04.pdf); [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 5–7](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf).
3. Make relational equality, equivalence-preserving transformations, and substitution-based verification explicit capabilities and validation diagnostics. An exercise should not be considered harder merely because it contains `=`; the model should identify what reasoning about equality it requires. [Knuth, Stephens, McNeil, and Alibali, 2006, pp. 297–312](https://psych.wisc.edu/alibali/home/Publications_files/Knuth_Stephens_McNeil_Alibali_JRME_2006.pdf); [Common Core State Standards, pp. 43, 51 and 64](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf).
4. Compute diagnostics from a normalized equation and a shortest valid pedagogical solution path, not only from the generation trace. At minimum expose required transformations, path length, unknown occurrences and sides, term count, nesting depth, sign profile, and whether a materially shorter path makes the exercise trivial. This is a design inference from evidence that particular transformations change difficulty across structures and that interacting elements raise load. [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 3 and 6–7](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf); [Ngu, Chung, and Yeung, 2015](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1056287).
5. Treat representation as a selectable capability and calibration dimension. If the first product increment presents only symbolic equations, record that restriction explicitly and do not generalize its calibrated difficulty to verbal, contextual, tabular, or graphical tasks. [Koedinger and Nathan, 2004, pp. 142–144](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Koedinger%2CNathan-04.pdf); [Common Core State Standards, pp. 43, 51 and 54](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf).
6. Introduce negative constants, negative coefficients, variable terms on both sides, distribution, and systems as separately observable transitions. A level may combine previously mastered dimensions, but generated samples and distributions must reveal which transition causes the added demand. [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 5–7](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf).
7. For this first increment, enforce integer inputs and answers, exact division, later introduction of negatives, and exactly one solution as hard eligibility rules rather than difficulty signals. These are project scope constraints; keeping them separate prevents an invalid or incompatible exercise from receiving a plausible difficulty score.
8. Use a two-equation, two-variable system as the first multi-unknown endpoint. This is the common first systems target in both standards and supports algebraic and graphical interpretations; preserve `equation_count` and `variable_count` as separate schema dimensions so a later effort can investigate larger systems without pretending their progression has already been validated. [Common Core State Standards, pp. 51 and 54](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf); [Department for Education, key-stage 3 algebra](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study).

## Reference nine-stage action spine

This is a research-backed starting hypothesis for later calibration, not a claim that standards mandate nine levels. It fits the repository's existing nine-level shape while keeping the stages named by learner action.

| Stage | Dominant learner action | Representative structure | Important controlled dimensions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Evaluate one operation | `a + b`, `a - b` | Nonnegative integers; result known |
| 2 | Evaluate composed arithmetic | `(a + b) × c`, exact `a ÷ b` | Operation order, nesting, exact division |
| 3 | Find a missing value and verify equality | `□ + b = c`, `a × □ = c`, including unknown in different positions | Unknown position, inverse-operation family, relational `=` |
| 4 | Read and evaluate a variable expression | `2(x + 3)` for a supplied `x`; recognize an equivalent form | Substitution, coefficient/term vocabulary, simple distribution |
| 5 | Solve and verify a one-step equation | `x + b = c`, `ax = c` | One unknown occurrence, one inverse transformation |
| 6 | Solve a two-step equation | `ax + b = c` | Ordered inverse transformations; introduce negative constants or solutions only here or later |
| 7 | Simplify, then solve | `a(x + b) + c = d` or like terms on one side | Distribution, collection, nesting, longer minimal path |
| 8 | Reconcile variable terms on both sides | `ax + b = cx + d` | Variable-term transformation, signs, unique solution after normalization |
| 9 | Coordinate two linear constraints | two independent equations in `x` and `y` | Substitution or elimination; two equations, two variables, unique integer solution |

The spine follows the standards' movement from computation, to expressions and equivalence, to simple and multi-step one-variable equations, to expansion/collection and pairs of simultaneous equations. Its deliberate separation of unknown position, variable-side structure, negatives, and representation is supported by the empirical studies above. [Common Core State Standards, pp. 27–28, 43, 48 and 51–55](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Math_Standards1.pdf); [Koedinger and Nathan, 2004, pp. 142–144](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Koedinger%2CNathan-04.pdf); [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 2–7](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf).

## Implications for later evaluation work

An equation gallery should display the action stage and all orthogonal diagnostics beside each sample, and distribution views should summarize them separately. Human review can then distinguish “wrong stage” from “bad number mix,” “awkward representation,” “too many interacting elements,” or “trivial because of a shortcut.” This recommendation follows from the finding that representation, unknown position, number type, sign, and equation structure have distinct effects and should therefore not disappear into one opaque difficulty score. [Koedinger and Nathan, 2004, pp. 142–144](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Koedinger%2CNathan-04.pdf); [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 5–7](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf).

The research does not supply universal numeric weights or prove that these nine adjacent stages will feel equally spaced for this product's learners. Those values must be calibrated against generated samples and observed learner or human-review evidence. Koedinger and Nathan's counterintuitive representation result and Long and colleagues' evidence of under-generalized knowledge are reasons to keep these as testable hypotheses rather than hard-code expert intuition as fact. [Koedinger and Nathan, 2004, pp. 142–144](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Koedinger%2CNathan-04.pdf); [Long, Holstein, and Aleven, 2018, pp. 6–7](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED662738.pdf).
